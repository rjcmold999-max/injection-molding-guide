# CNC Machining vs. Injection Molding for Plastic Parts

CNC machining and injection molding can both produce functional plastic parts. The right choice depends on the number of parts needed, the time available, design stability, and the total cost of the project.

CNC machining cuts parts from solid plastic stock without a mold. Injection molding requires a mold upfront, then produces repeatable parts at a lower cost per unit as volume increases.

---

## Process Overview

### 1. CNC Machining
Machine parts directly from plastic stock. With no mold to build, it is useful for prototypes, functional tests, urgent orders, and designs that may change. Each additional part still takes machine time and material.

### 2. Rapid Tooling + Injection Molding
Build a lower-cost mold for a pilot run. Tooling takes time and money upfront, but molding can reduce the unit cost and provide consistent parts once the design is ready.

### 3. Production Tooling + Injection Molding
Invest in a production mold for sustained, higher-volume demand. The larger tooling investment is spread across more parts, reducing the effective cost per part.

---

## How to Choose

| Method | Typical project stage | Upfront tooling | Illustrative lead time | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CNC machining | Dozens of parts | None | 3–7 days | Prototypes, testing, fast design changes |
| Rapid tooling + injection molding | Hundreds to a few thousand parts | Required | 2–4 weeks | Pilot runs and market trials |
| Production tooling + injection molding | Thousands of parts and ongoing demand | Higher investment | 4–6 weeks | Repeatable production at scale |

These ranges are examples from the source article, not fixed cutoffs. Actual costs and schedules depend on the material, geometry, tolerances, finish, tooling, and supplier capacity.

---

## Why Teams Often Use Both

- **Start with CNC** when speed and the ability to revise the design matter most.
- **Move to rapid tooling** when the design stabilizes and a pilot run needs consistent parts.
- **Use production tooling** when demand supports the larger mold investment.

For example, the source article follows a medical-device housing from 50 CNC prototypes to a 300-part pilot run and then to production of 5,000 or more parts.

---

## Engineering Insight

Compare the **total project cost**, not just the quoted price per part:

`CNC total = CNC unit cost × quantity`

`Molding total = tooling cost + molded unit cost × quantity`

For one illustrative part in the source article, CNC machining costs $60–$100 per part; rapid tooling costs $3,000–$5,000, followed by $2–$5 per molded part. The break-even quantity changes with the actual quotes and any revisions to the mold. A CNC prototype can help validate the design before committing to tooling.

# What Is Plastic Injection Molding Process?

Plastic injection molding is a manufacturing process used to produce plastic parts by injecting molten material into a precision-machined mold.

It is widely used in automotive, medical, electronics, and industrial applications because it enables high-volume production with consistent quality and low unit cost.

---

## Process Overview

The injection molding cycle consists of four main stages:

### 1. Clamping
The mold is closed under high pressure to prepare for injection.

### 2. Injection
Molten plastic is injected into the mold cavity under controlled pressure and temperature.

### 3. Cooling
The material cools and solidifies inside the mold, forming the final part geometry.

### 4. Ejection
The mold opens, and ejector pins push the finished part out. The cycle then repeats.

---

## Why Injection Molding Is Widely Used

- High repeatability for mass production
- Fast cycle time (seconds to minutes)
- Ability to produce complex geometries
- Wide range of engineering plastics (ABS, PC, POM, PP, etc.)
- Low cost per part at scale

---

## Engineering Insight

Injection molding is not just a simple filling process.

Part quality depends on a balance of:

- Mold design
- Material selection
- Injection pressure
- Temperature control
- Cooling time

Small deviations in these parameters can lead to defects such as:

- Sink marks  
- Warpage  
- Short shots  
- Flash
